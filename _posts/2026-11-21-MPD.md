# MPD

<span class="publish-date"> Published on  {{ page.date | date: "%d %B %Y" }}

Masochistic personality disorder is a psychopathology where a person frequently behaves in self-defeating ways, which predictably increase their chance of suffering. The motive for this might vary, but generally does not seem to be due to a love of suffering for its own sake, but rather as a means to an end. Some of these ends are:

1. Exhibitionism (trying to draw attention and attract sympathy for oneself)
2. Moral narcissism (suffering is evidence of virtue, and so those who suffer the most are superior to others)
3. Deflection of guilt (using past suffering to exculpate oneself)

If you haven’t heard about this personality disorder, it’s because feminists opposed its inclusion in the DSM. It had been commonly observed for decades that a significant number of women in abusive relationships were masochists: they intentionally provoked their partners, derived some sort of satisfaction from the abuse they received, and if one partner left them, they would seek out another equally abusive one. Erin Pizzey, who founded the women’s refuge movement, estimated that 62% of women in her refuge were ‘violence-prone’.

So the whole diagnosis was thrown out for political reasons, as it was construed as a facilitator of ‘victim-blaming’. But even putting aside the question of domestic violence, it had been observed by Freud that masochism was associated more with women than men, and this likely also played a role in feminist opposition.

If you read the literature, it’s somewhat incredible that the diagnosis was ever dropped. Consider this quote from McWilliams, who has a section on the condition in ‘Psychoanalytic Diagnosis’ (2011).

> “Often they are much more interested in winning a moral victory than in solving a practical problem. It took me weeks of work to get one self-defeating patient to consider writing a letter to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) that would get her the large refund to which she was legally entitled. She spent her therapy hours trying to convince me that the IRS had handled her tax return ineptly - which was emphatically true but completely beside the point if the point was to get her money back. She much preferred my sympathetic indignation to my attempts to help her get recompensed. Left to herself, she would have gone on collecting and bemoaning injustices rather than eliminating one.”

If that doesn’t sound like half the population, I don’t know what does. Similarly, the phenomenon where women martyr themselves doing often trivial amounts of housework, while rejecting assistance (and getting angry at the offer) is another textbook symptom. This is known as ‘injustice collecting’: the patient is constantly in search of ways to demonstrate they’ve been wronged, which they can then use to demand sympathy or make their partner feel guilty (or excuse cheating).

Another example, already alluded to somewhat, is the woman who has inveterate ‘bad luck’ with men, despite forever choosing those who are most likely to mistreat her. The physically abusive relationship is only the tip of the iceberg here; being strung along in situationships by men who don’t respect her is far more common.

Finally, it’s worth noting the clinically observed relationship between MPD and Munchausen’s, now known as factitious disorder imposed on self. Around two thirds of clinical cases are women, although I imagine that proportion would blow out if one included non-clinical cases, i.e. the great number of women self-diagnosing on the internet. While the suffering of these patients is obvious, the underlying motivation again seems to be to elicit sympathy and to find an excuse for one’s future behaviour (e.g. poor performance at work).

Now my real interest here is not resurrecting a useful diagnosis. It’s that I’m forever interested in the relationship between the psychopathology of the individual and that of society more broadly. I think it’s fairly obvious that, as Western society has feminized, it has itself become masochistic. And as society has become masochistic, it’s also encouraged that thought pattern and behaviour in individuals susceptible to it. Indeed I don’t think you could add the condition into the DSM nowadays because a personality disorder is definitionally ‘abnormal’ and yet masochism has been thoroughly normalized and is now ubiquitous.

So what does a masochistic culture look like?

1. Suffering indicative of virtue (e.g. oppression olympics)
2. Repeated societal self-flagellation (e.g. over past historical ills)
3. Suicidal empathy (e.g. serial violent offenders being released onto the streets)
4. The victim is always right (any questioning of the victim is a form of ‘victim-blaming’)
5. Rampant psychogenic illness, especially self-diagnosed mental illness
6. External locus of control (one’s actions are due to the system, one’s past trauma etc.)
7. Empathy > Rationality (‘read the room’, caring about the truth = autism)

The list speaks for itself.

What about the prognosis and treatment? MPD has always posed a serious problem in a therapeutic setting in that patients do not want to get better; they want to be the sort of person who suffers and thus has to go to therapy where they receive lots of sympathy, and so they sabotage any attempt to get better.

The tips for dealing with these patients are instructive, though:

1. Do not express sympathy for the patient; if they raise a problem, calmly discuss potential solutions rather than focusing on their suffering.
2. Internalise their locus of control; gently remind them that they are responsible for their own actions.
3. Deglamorise suffering; pointless suffering is bad and should be avoided.

The patient will resist all these steps, hence the difficulty in treatment. I am very sceptical that society will adopt these principles, which is to say, ignoring women’s tears and making them accountable for their actions, or suggesting that some victims choose to be victims. I certainly can’t see it happening in the near future. Probably the most likely prospect for a reversal is if women in their increasing lunacy embrace a psychogenic epidemic so implausible that the health establishment is forced to make a stand (due to budgetary pressures on governments which won’t be able to cope with all women claiming one ailment or another, in conjunction with an aging population).
